# Eight-week study plan

Plan for three or four study sessions per week, adapting to your experience. A session can be 45–90 minutes. Progress by demonstrated understanding rather than a fixed deadline.

| Week | Modules | Evidence of learning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 00–02: setup, types, variables/functions | Deploy twice; explain persistence, units and visibility |
| 2 | 03–04: decisions and arrays | Test thresholds; distinguish delete from pop |
| 3 | 05–06: loops and mappings | Trace all loop forms; distinguish missing from zero |
| 4 | 07–08: records and locations | Enforce transitions; predict copy/alias behavior |
| 5 | 09–10: access, errors and events | Test two callers, decoded logs and rollback |
| 6 | 11–12: inheritance and composition | Explain override versus calling another instance |
| 7 | 13–15: Ether, calls and encoding | Test rejected payments, immediate callers and commitments |
| 8 | 16–17 and capstones | Show bounded progress, conservation and complete project tests |

## Use this checklist for each topic

- [ ] I can explain the concept without reading the lesson.
- [ ] I predicted the example's outputs before running it.
- [ ] I ran normal, boundary and invalid inputs.
- [ ] I attempted all three exercises before looking at solutions.
- [ ] I scored at least 3/4 on the quiz and can explain every answer.
- [ ] I recorded one mistake and a test that catches it.

## Completion criteria

Complete all 54 exercise specifications, all quizzes and at least one capstone. For a stronger portfolio, complete all three capstones and include your own tests and design explanation. You should be able to identify the remaining limitations rather than describing a teaching example as a production application.
